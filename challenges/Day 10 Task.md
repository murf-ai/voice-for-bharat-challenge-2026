# Day 10 – Share Your Voice Agent Journey

Over the last nine days, you built a voice agent that can talk, follow guardrails, remember users, use tools, make outbound calls, ask humans for help, track call outcomes, and hand conversations to a specialist. Today, you will share what you learned so someone else can build one too.

For Day 10, your objective is to:

- **Step 1: Choose the format for your blog post.** You can write:
  - A story about what you built during the challenge
  - A step-by-step guide to building a voice agent
  - A combination of both

  Your post does not need to document every change from every day. Keep it simple, useful, and based on your own project.

- **Step 2: Introduce your agent.** Explain the problem it solves, the track you chose, who it is for, and why voice is useful for those users.

- **Step 3: Describe the important features you built.** Choose the features that best tell your project's story. These could include:
  - An Indian voice powered by **Murf Falcon**
  - A clear personality, objectives, and safety guardrails
  - Support for Indian languages or code-mixed conversations
  - A frontend that shows the agent's current state
  - Memory for returning users
  - Tools that fetch or compute useful data
  - Outbound phone calls
  - Human escalation
  - A call analytics dashboard
  - Handoffs to specialist agents

- **Step 4: Write about the difficult parts.** Share one or more problems you faced, what caused them, what you tried, and how you solved or worked around them. Real lessons are more useful than pretending everything worked on the first attempt.

- **Step 5: Help the reader build their own voice agent.** Include enough practical guidance for someone to begin. At minimum, explain:
  - The main components: speech-to-text, an LLM, text-to-speech, and real-time transport
  - How to set up and run your project
  - Where to add API keys without exposing them
  - How to connect to the agent and test a conversation

  Link to your public repository so readers can inspect the code. Never publish API keys, phone numbers, caller data, or other private information.

- **Step 6: Add evidence from your build.** Include screenshots, short clips, architecture diagrams, code snippets, or links to your earlier demos where they help explain the project. You do not need to include all of them.

- **Step 7: Publish the post.** You can use [DEV Community](https://dev.to/), Hashnode, Medium, your own website, or any other public blogging platform.

- **Step 8: Share the published blog on LinkedIn.** Briefly describe what you built and what you learned. Mention that you built your voice agent using the fastest TTS API — **Murf Falcon**. Mention that you completed **10 Days of Voice Agents — VoiceForBharat Edition**, tag the official **Murf AI** handle, and use the hashtag **#VoiceForBharat**.

- **Step 9: Submit your LinkedIn post link** using the submission form shared on Discord.

## Suggested Blog Structure

1. The problem and the users
2. What the voice agent does
3. How the system works
4. The most important features
5. Challenges and how you overcame them
6. How readers can build and run it
7. What you would improve next
8. Links to the code and demos

## Advanced (Optional)

You only need a clear, useful public blog post to complete Day 10. Try these if you want to do more:

- Add a simple architecture diagram showing how audio moves through your system.
- Include a short code example for your agent session, a tool, or a handoff.
- Measure and share latency, call success rate, or another result from your dashboard.
- Explain one design decision you changed during the challenge and why.
- Add a short troubleshooting section for a problem other builders may encounter.

### You've finished Day 10 if:

- Your blog explains what your agent does and who it helps
- It covers important features from your build without needing to list every day
- It honestly describes at least one difficulty and how you handled it
- It gives readers a practical starting point for building their own voice agent
- It links to your public repository and does not expose private information
- Your blog is published, and your LinkedIn post is live

Once your blog is published, your LinkedIn post is live, and your form submission is complete, you have completed **10 Days of Voice Agents — VoiceForBharat Edition**.
